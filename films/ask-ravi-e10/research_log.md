## Open questions
- f2: (Non-blocking) 'Which planet spins slowest? Venus!' depends on comparing all eight planets. The quote covers only Mercury and Venus (with f1 adding Earth, Mars and Jupiter). Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are on the page but not quoted. Suggested: Add this passage, checked on the page: 'Saturn takes 11 hours, Uranus takes 17 hours, and Neptune takes 16 hours.'
- f1: (Non-blocking) The claim says 'Earth spins in 24 hours', but the same page says one full spin (sidereal day) is 'almost exactly 23 hours and 56 minutes', and that solar days vary by a few minutes. 24 hours is the solar day. Ravi's 'Only on Earth!' is an acceptable simplification and does not itself say 'spin'. Suggested: Reword the claim to 'A day on Earth lasts about 24 hours; Mars about 25; Jupiter spins in about 10.' Optionally change the line to 'Only on Earth, more or less!'

## Counter-evidence
- For Venus and Mercury, 'spin once' and 'one day' are very different. NASA's Venus facts page (https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/) gives Venus's rotation as 243 Earth days (= 5,832 h, consistent with Space Place). It says sunrise to sunset 'would take 117 Earth days', and that Venus spins backward. Ravi's line 2 rightly says 'to spin round just once', not 'a day lasts', so it is correct. Do not reword it to 'a day on Venus is 5,832 hours'.
