## What it teaches
1. **A wordless action beat** (8 s) in the middle lets sound effects and motion carry the story, then the dialogue resumes.
2. **The tool as a character** whose intent is misread (the tree thinks it's being felled) is a cheap, strong hook.
3. **Payoff → new worry → fix:** straightening succeeds, then the wounds become the next problem, which keeps attention through the middle.
4. Consistent channel format: skit → time card → explainer duo; this reel's explainer is 22 s.

## Fact check (for our own use)
Not verified, and I would not reuse the claim as stated. The explainer says the cuts go on the *inside* of the bend
while part of the wood stays intact. Mechanically, straightening lengthens the concave (inside) face and shortens the
convex face, so relief cuts that close up would sit on the convex side; cuts on the inside would open. Extension
guidance for leaning trees generally favours staking young trees and warns that trunk wounds invite decay. Any film on
this needs a horticultural source first.
